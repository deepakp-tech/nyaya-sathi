## Evidence to collect
- Rent agreement (registered or not), deposit payment proof (bank/UPI/cheque/receipt)
- Proof of vacating: handover message, key return, move-out photos/video
- All messages with the landlord about the deposit and any claimed damage
- Unpaid bills settled (electricity, maintenance) — receipts

## Steps, cheapest first
1. Polite written request (WhatsApp/email) with a clear deadline (7 days). Keep proof.
2. Legal notice by registered/speed post: facts, amount, deadline (usually 15 days), next step. Can be sent by the person or an advocate.
3. If the state has a Rent Authority under a Model-Tenancy-based law, apply there.
4. Otherwise a civil money-recovery suit (small amounts: local civil court). Check the 3-year limitation.
5. Free legal aid via the District Legal Services Authority if eligible.

## Common landlord arguments and replies
- "Painting/cleaning" — normal wear and tear is usually not deductible unless the agreement says so.
- "Damage" — ask for itemised proof (bills, photos). Share your move-out photos.
- "Will pay after new tenant comes" — the deposit is owed on vacating, not on re-letting, unless agreed.

## When to get a lawyer
- Amount is large, the landlord has filed a case against you, or more than ~2.5 years have passed.
